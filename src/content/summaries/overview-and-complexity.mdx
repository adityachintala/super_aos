- A **system** is an interrelated set of components with an identifiable boundary, working together for a purpose. It has nine characteristics: components, interrelationships, boundary, purpose, environment, interfaces, input, output and constraints.
- Every tool has a machine part and an operating part. The **operating system** is the operating part: it hides the machine's details behind simple services.
- A computer system has four components: **hardware, operating system, application programs, users**. The operating system is an intermediary whose goals are **convenience** and **efficiency**; it is a **resource allocator**, a **control program**, and at its core the **kernel**.
- **Signs of complexity**: many components, many interconnections, many irregularities, a long description, a team of maintainers.
- **Sources**: cascading and interacting requirements (escalating complexity), generality, scaling, change over time, and maintaining high utilisation.
- **Coping**: modularity (N² becomes N²/K), abstraction (external specifications only; the robustness principle), layering, hierarchy.
- Computer systems have **no nearby bounds on composition** (digital, and controlled by software) and an **unprecedented rate of technological change**. So designers also **iterate** from a simple working system, and **keep it simple**.
