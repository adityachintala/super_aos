- A **race condition** arises when several processes manipulate shared data concurrently and the result depends on the order. Even `counter++` is three instructions.
- The **critical-section problem** asks for a protocol with **mutual exclusion**, **progress** and **bounded waiting**.

| Attempt | Mutual exclusion | Progress | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algorithm 1 (turn) | Yes | No: strict alternation | Fails |
| Algorithm 2 (flags) | Yes | No: both can wait for ever | Fails |
| Algorithm 3 (Peterson) | Yes | Yes, with bounded waiting | Correct |

- The **bakery algorithm** extends this to *n* processes with numbered tickets.
- **Hardware**: disabling interrupts (uniprocessor only), and the atomic **TestAndSet** and **Swap**. They are simple and general but involve **busy waiting**, and may starve.
- A **mutex lock** is acquire and release; a **spinlock** busy-waits and suits short waits on multiprocessors.
- A **semaphore** has atomic **wait** and **signal**. Initialised to 1 it gives mutual exclusion; to 0, ordering; to *n*, a count of resources. The blocking implementation queues waiting processes, and a negative value counts them. Misordered waits cause **deadlock**.
- **Bounded buffer**: mutex = 1, empty = *n*, full = 0. **Readers-writers**: the first reader in and last reader out handle `wrt`. **Dining philosophers**: the simple solution deadlocks; limit to four, take both chopsticks atomically, or make one asymmetric.
- A **monitor** enforces mutual exclusion automatically and uses **condition variables**, whose signal is lost if nobody is waiting.
